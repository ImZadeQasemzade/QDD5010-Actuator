Drive

Moves the joint. Pick a mode with the tabs; sending a command switches the motor into that mode.
Angle: goes to an output angle and holds it (firmware B). Set zero makes the current position read 0° (Z). It is forgotten at power-off, because the encoder only knows the motor angle within one turn.
Speed: spins the output continuously at an RPM (S). Stop holds speed 0.
Torque: pushes with a set current in amps (T), whatever the arm does. Release sets 0 A: the arm goes free but stays powered. The hint uses the calibration: on a load, torque ≈ Kt × (I − push friction).
Enable (top bar) powers the motor and holds where the arm is. STOP or Esc cuts power so the arm goes limp.

Force

What is pressing on the arm from outside. FREE means the current is what free motion needs. CONTACT means something is pushing back, and the numbers show how hard: output torque in N·m, and the force in grams at the lever length (Setup → Calibration values → Lever).
Held against a load, it uses the scale calibration, Kt × (I − push friction), good to about ±8 g at 100 mm between 1 and 3.5 A. While moving, it compares the current with the friction learned by Learn free motion (Setup → Contact detection), so run that first if you want contacts during moves.

Log

Everything sent to the board (blue, starting with >) and everything it replies. Type a raw firmware command in the box and press Enter: H lists them all, G shows gains, limits and calibration. Telemetry lines (20 per second) are hidden unless ticked.

Limits

Current cap limits the motor current in every mode (C, max 4 A). Voltage cap limits the voltage on the winding (QV, max 3 V). It is a backstop and must stay high enough for the current: about 0.7 V per amp cold, 0.85 V warm, plus a little for speed. Power-up defaults: 3.5 A and 3.0 V. The driver chip has no heatsink, so keep long pushes near the cap short.

Calibration values

Kt: output torque per extra amp. Push friction: current the gearbox uses up before any torque reaches a load. Together: torque ≈ Kt × (I − push friction) when pushing on something. From the 2026-11-08 scale runs: 0.099 N·m/A and 0.87 A. Lever: distance from the axis to where the force is read, used to turn N·m into grams (100 mm for Arm A1 with the pusher).

Contact detection

Learn free motion turns the arm a full circle each way at 5 RPM, then makes a few ±40° steps. From that it learns how much current free motion needs at every angle (friction) and when speeding up (inertia). The arm must be free to swing all the way round. Threshold is how far above that the current must go to count as contact; learning sets it from the noise. Untick on to stop flagging contacts.

Torque calibration

Measures Kt and push friction with a kitchen scale. Clamp the base so it cannot twist, stand the scale on its edge against a wall and rest the pusher tip on it at the lever length. STOP, tare, then Enable and go down the rows in order without letting the arm back off: Apply, wait a second, type the grams, Save. Going only upward keeps friction always against the push, so the fit's offset is the push friction. Use fit copies the result into Calibration values. Prefilled with the 2026-11-08 runs.

Display and recording

Flip reverses the 3D arm's direction if it turns opposite to the real one. Axis horizontal lays the 3D actuator on its side to match a sideways mount. Record CSV saves every telemetry sample (angle, speed, currents, voltages, mode, target, outside torque, contact) until you press it again.
